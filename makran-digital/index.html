CV / RÉSUMÉ

What a strong CV should include

Contact details, short profile, education, relevant skills, projects or experience, achievements and references only when requested. Keep it honest, readable and tailored to the opportunity.

MOTIVATION LETTER

Explain your purpose clearly

State what you are applying for, why the programme fits your goals, what evidence shows your preparation, and how you plan to use the opportunity. Avoid generic copied paragraphs.

RECOMMENDATION LETTER

Ask the right person early

Ask a teacher, supervisor or mentor who knows your work. Share the programme details, your achievements and the deadline. Never write or sign a letter in someone else's name.

Important document safety

Upload documents only to the official university, scholarship or employer portal. Never send passwords, OTPs or bank PINs. Do not pay an agent to “guarantee” a scholarship or job. Keep clear scans and never alter marksheets, certificates or identity documents.

05 / TEST PREPARATION
Important: practice material, not an official paper.

This starter quiz is independently written for general practice. It is not an official USAT, USAT-E, MDCAT, ECAT or university past paper, and it does not claim to reproduce any exact test pattern. Always check the latest syllabus, weightage and dates with the test provider.

A SIMPLE WEEKLY ROUTINE

Study with consistency.

Day 1Verbal ability: vocabulary, grammar and reading comprehension.
Day 2Quantitative reasoning: percentages, ratios, algebra and arithmetic.
Day 3Analytical reasoning: sequences, logic, patterns and deductions.
Day 4Review the subject topics in your own test's official syllabus.
Day 5Attempt a timed mixed practice set without checking answers.
Day 6Review mistakes and revise the weakest two topics.
Day 7Rest, read in English and plan the next study week.

06 / CAREER GUIDANCE
Your subjects do not decide your whole future.

Use these profiles as starting points. Admission rules, merit, subject combinations, licensing and test requirements vary by university and year. Confirm every requirement with the relevant university or professional regulator before applying.

01 · MEDICAL & HEALTH SCIENCES

Careers in health

For students interested in patient care, laboratory work, medicines, rehabilitation and public health.

Explore degrees and pathways
  • Degrees: MBBS, BDS, BS Nursing, Pharm-D, DPT, Medical Laboratory Technology, Biotechnology and Public Health.
  • Intermediate background: FSc Pre-Medical is commonly required for MBBS/BDS and many health programmes. Other degrees may accept different subject combinations; check each university.
  • Admission: MDCAT is required for MBBS/BDS under applicable rules. Other programmes may use university tests, merit or interviews.
  • Skills: Biology, chemistry, communication, empathy, careful observation and ethical practice.
  • Career routes: Hospitals, clinics, laboratories, pharmacies, research, rehabilitation and public-health organisations. Regulated professions require the relevant registration/licensing.
  • Next steps: Check subject eligibility → compare recognised programmes → prepare for the required test → apply → build practical and communication skills.
02 · ICS & COMPUTER SCIENCES

Careers in technology

For students who enjoy computing, problem-solving, software, data and digital systems.

Explore degrees and pathways
  • Degrees: BS Computer Science, Software Engineering, Artificial Intelligence, Information Technology, Cybersecurity, Data Science and Computer Engineering.
  • Intermediate background: ICS is a common route. FSc Pre-Engineering is widely accepted; some universities accept Pre-Medical with additional mathematics or bridging requirements.
  • Admission: University entry tests may include mathematics, analytical reasoning and English. Subject requirements differ by programme.
  • Skills: Mathematics, programming fundamentals, logical thinking, communication and project-building.
  • Career routes: Software developer, web/mobile developer, data analyst, AI/ML roles, cybersecurity, IT support, QA and systems roles.
  • Next steps: Strengthen mathematics → learn one programming language → build small projects → compare accredited degrees → create a portfolio.
03 · ARTS & HUMANITIES

Careers in people, ideas & culture

For students drawn to language, creativity, society, history, communication and public affairs.

Explore degrees and pathways
  • Degrees: English, Psychology, Sociology, International Relations, Political Science, History, Fine Arts, Media Studies and Communication.
  • Intermediate background: FA and other equivalent qualifications are common routes; many universities accept a range of Intermediate groups.
  • Admission: University merit, aptitude tests, interviews or portfolio reviews may apply, especially for creative programmes.
  • Skills: Writing, research, critical thinking, public speaking, creativity and cultural awareness.
  • Career routes: Teaching (with required qualifications), media, research, communications, public service, NGOs, policy and creative industries.
  • Next steps: Explore subjects → read widely → develop writing/research samples → check degree criteria → build experience through projects or volunteering.
04 · COMMERCE & BUSINESS

Careers in business & finance

For students interested in organisations, markets, money, management and entrepreneurship.

Explore degrees and pathways
  • Degrees: BBA, Accounting & Finance, Economics, Marketing, Entrepreneurship, Banking and Business Analytics.
  • Intermediate background: I.Com is a direct route for many programmes; FA, ICS and FSc students may also qualify depending on university policy.
  • Admission: University tests may assess English, mathematics, analytical reasoning and general knowledge.
  • Skills: Numeracy, spreadsheets, communication, teamwork, analysis and ethical decision-making.
  • Career routes: Accounting, banking, finance, sales, marketing, HR, operations, business analysis and starting a business.
  • Next steps: Build numeracy and digital skills → compare degree specialisations → practise spreadsheets → seek projects or internships.
05 · ENGINEERING & NATURAL SCIENCES

Careers in science & engineering

For students interested in designing, building, investigating and understanding the natural world.

Explore degrees and pathways
  • Degrees: Electrical, Civil, Mechanical and Chemical Engineering; Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and related sciences.
  • Intermediate background: FSc Pre-Engineering is a common engineering route. Subject combinations for science degrees vary by institution.
  • Admission: Engineering programmes may require an accepted entry test such as the university's own test or another recognised option under current rules.
  • Skills: Mathematics, physics, problem-solving, technical drawing, experimentation and teamwork.
  • Career routes: Engineering design, construction, energy, manufacturing, laboratories, research and technical services.
  • Next steps: Review mathematics/physics → check PEC and university requirements where applicable → prepare for the accepted test → build practical skills.
06 · EDUCATION & SOCIAL SCIENCES

Careers in learning & community

For students who want to teach, support communities, study society or improve public services.

Explore degrees and pathways
  • Degrees: Education, B.Ed pathways, Special Education, Social Work, Development Studies and Public Administration.
  • Intermediate background: FA, I.Com, ICS and FSc students may be eligible for many programmes; verify the university's subject rules.
  • Admission: Usually university merit or an entry test; professional teaching roles may require additional recognised qualifications.
  • Skills: Communication, patience, planning, research, leadership and community engagement.
  • Career routes: Teaching, education management, social services, development organisations, public administration and programme coordination.
  • Next steps: Identify the population or issue you want to work with → compare degrees → volunteer or tutor → gain research and communication skills.
